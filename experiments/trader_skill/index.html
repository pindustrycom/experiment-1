trader-skill, tested

Can you beat the market by copying famous investors or members of Congress? A backtest of the two signals in Jake Schincariol's trader-skill Claude skills, using its own SEC and House readers.

Verdict: no tested edge. Copying 23 famous funds' 13F filings matched SPY, and copying every stock buy the House discloses trailed it. Pelosi's own buys are the one outlier, carried by a few big tech positions. The repo is genuine and safe (public data only, never places orders); the rest of it is tools for running a real brokerage account, which this paper-only project does not need.

Copying famous investors (13F)

Funds over $100M file their US stock holdings every quarter, up to 45 days after it ends. The copy buys each fund's 10 biggest stock positions (weighted by value) the trading day after the filing appears, holds until the next one, and pays 5 bp a side. The last column enters at the quarter end instead, which nobody could do: the gap is what the delay costs.

What to keep in mind

Prices come from Yahoo, which has no delisted stocks, so both tests flatter the strategies a little. Pelosi's numbers rest on one person and about 60 trades, mostly a few large tech positions held a year; she often buys call options, and the test copies with shares. Her disclosed buys are already traded on paper as the Pelosi pot on the Markets page.
