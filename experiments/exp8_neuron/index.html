Exp8 · Joaquin Philco's resonant neuron · 20–21 Oct 2026

A neuron with myelin resonators, and a volume of 4,096 of them

Joaquin Philco's neuron design: a soma that decides in time with a sigmoid, one axon per connection, myelin sheaths that are resonators, and oligodendrocytes that read the sheaths and set the weight where each axon meets the next cell's dendrite. First one neuron wired to two others, running live; then a 16 × 16 × 16 volume of them trained only by oligodendrocytes, on digits and colours.

Result so far. Oligodendrocytes that only check rhythm and lock leave the volume no better than frozen (44.8% vs 43.5%). Adding Joaquin's resonance-index sleep, which replays each class, prunes the axons that carry the least of their target's ringing and turns the most anti-resonant ones inhibitory, lifts it to 72.6% and makes suppression real (18% of axons). The learned structures sit at the input face, and a new class learns twice as well as in the frozen volume (50.7% vs 24.7% with 5 shots) but cannot yet reach the freed space deeper in.

Choices made where the design left it open

You wrote that the axon does the sum of the weighted inputs. I put that sum where the dendrites meet the soma, and let each axon carry its own soma's output out to one target, because you also said each connection is an axon.

You didn't say what the oligodendrocyte looks for, so I gave it two readings. On its own axon it rewards a clear rhythm over noise. Where one oligodendrocyte wraps two axons going into the same cell, as in the brain where one oligodendrocyte myelinates several axons, it also compares them: axons ringing in phase strengthen together, out of phase weaken. That second reading is where co-resonance between structures could live. Weights can go negative, which is suppression.

Unlike the lattice cell, this soma is a bounded sigmoid with no self-feedback, so the cells can't run away into saturation.

2 · The full test: a 16 × 16 × 16 volume

4,096 of these neurons, each sending an axon to each of its 26 neighbours. The image arrives on the front face (z = 0) through opponent colour and ON/OFF channels. One oligodendrocyte sits on each 2 × 2 × 2 block and latches onto every axon ending there. It checks rhythm, suppresses axons out of phase with its block, keeps a fixed weight budget, and after each class runs the resonance-index sleep. Without the sleep, every cell is driven by the same image, so the axons into a block almost always ring in phase and the oligodendrocytes strengthen everything. The sleep replays 50 images of the class just learned and gives each axon its resonance index: its signed share of its target cell's ringing. The 2% with the smallest index are pruned (the bridge is broken) and the 2% most anti-resonant compared with noise become inhibitory. That quiets the deep volume, which had been blurring what the face sees.

The trained volume learns the new class about twice as well and keeps the old ones, but the new class also lands on the face. Lifting suppression along the new input's path did not help: the input lights only about 3% of cells, all within two slices of the face, because the sigmoid signal fades before it gets deeper. The next step is a growth front, where a new input strengthens its own axons inward until a deep cell fires and claims it.
