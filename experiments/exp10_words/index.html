Meet a new colour

A patch evokes the colour word it fits best, and that word predicts which colour cells should ring. When the prediction misses by more than it ever does on the five taught colours (the red line, the 95th percentile), the model asks "what is this?". Cyan, magenta and orange were seen while the colour cells formed but never named. Plum, gold and brown were never seen at all. Pick one of them as the colour above to read a phrase with a word that has no word cell yet.

The building blocks

1 · Letters by tracing

A word is one image. The eye splits it at the gaps and follows each character's outline at a steady pace, so a letter becomes a signal over time: direction, turning, place, pen lifts. Branched resonant neurons, one cell per symbol, ring on their own rhythm.

signal_t = [cos θ, sin θ, Δθ, y, x, lift] 72 steps per character
2 · Word cells

The letter cells' scores, one letter per beat, feed word cells made of the same graded resonant branches, one branch per letter position. So NO and ON ring different cells.

3 · Colour and digit cells

Colour cells read hue-tuned inputs (twelve hues, six brightness levels, one grey) while the eye scans the patch. Digit cells are the letter cells for 0 to 9. Sleep prunes neurons whose resonance index is below zero.

4 · Bonds and grammar

Shown a word with its thing, the ringing word cell bonds to the winning thing cell. From phrase lessons the model learns that slot 1 talks about colour and slot 2 about digits.

A[w, t] += r_w · 1[t = winner] pick = argmax cos(scene, Σ_k gate_k ⊙ r_k A)
